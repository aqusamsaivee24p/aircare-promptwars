What does pollution do?

PM2.5

Tiny particles that can travel deep into the lungs and may enter the bloodstream. They are especially concerning during long exposure.

PM10

Dust and larger particles that can irritate the nose, throat and lungs. Sources include road dust and construction.

NO₂

A gas commonly produced by traffic and fuel combustion. It can irritate airways and worsen respiratory symptoms.

SO₂

A gas released mainly by burning sulfur-containing fuels and some industries. It can trigger breathing difficulty, especially in people with asthma.

Ground-level O₃

Forms when sunlight reacts with other pollutants. It can cause coughing, chest discomfort and reduced lung function.

CO

Carbon monoxide is an invisible, odourless gas from incomplete combustion. High exposure can prevent blood from carrying enough oxygen.

What should I do when air quality is poor?

  • Reduce strenuous outdoor exercise; choose a cleaner indoor place when possible.
  • People with asthma, heart or lung conditions, children and older adults should take extra care and follow their clinician’s advice.
  • Keep windows closed during nearby pollution peaks if indoor air is cleaner; ventilate when outdoor air improves.
  • If you must be outdoors in particle pollution, a well-fitting N95/FFP2 respirator can reduce inhaled particles. It does not protect against gases such as SO₂ or CO.
  • Avoid burning rubbish, leaves or biomass; never run a generator or vehicle in an enclosed space.
Safety: AQI guidance is general information, not a medical diagnosis. If you have severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, confusion or fainting, seek urgent medical help.

Nature’s pollution clues

Lichens

Lichens are partnerships between a fungus and a photosynthetic partner. Some are sensitive to air pollution, especially sulfur dioxide, so their presence and diversity can help scientists study long-term local air quality.

Lichens are biological indicators, not a precise real-time AQI meter. Species, climate, bark and moisture also affect where they grow.

Other bioindicators

Leaf injury, mosses and selected plants can provide clues about pollutant exposure. Scientists compare observations with controls and measured pollutant data before drawing conclusions.

Do not identify pollution levels from one plant or one lichen alone.

Cleaner choices: small actions, shared impact

Vehicle maintenance & PUC

Maintain your vehicle, avoid unnecessary idling, and keep its Pollution Under Control (PUC) certificate valid as required by local rules. Check renewal and validity through official transport services.

Official Parivahan portal ↗

Walk, cycle, share

For short safe trips, consider walking or cycling. Use public transport or carpool where practical. Electric vehicles have no tailpipe emissions, though electricity generation and manufacturing also have impacts.

Plant wisely

Support locally suitable native trees and shrubs, protect existing trees, and maintain green spaces. Plants help, but they cannot replace emission reductions.

Clean neighbourhood

Do not burn waste. Report open burning and dusty construction where local civic channels allow. Keep streets clean and support community monitoring.
